\begin{kieeabstract}
% English abstract and Korean introduction updated from the supplied draft.
\rev{Vision--language driving models explain their actions with Chain-of-Causation (CoC) text, but an explanation rarely states when the requirement it introduces ends. Read in order, an earlier obligation such as ``stop until the pedestrian has passed'' may still be active when a later CoC justifies proceeding by a different condition, such as a green light. We present a framework that links each obligation to its release conditions and condition-specific evidence, separates confirmed contradictions from insufficient release evidence, and reports the first issue event and the obligation involved. The checking rules are implemented as a UPPAAL observer model with explicit queries. Checker inputs are symbolic frames written by the authors; automatic extraction accuracy on natural CoC text is not evaluated. On 144 authored scenarios, an earlier single-obligation checker matched the expected decision in 96/144 cases; the condition-linked model developed from this failure analysis matched 144/144 on regression, as did a simple finite-state machine. On 81 two- and three-scene boundary cases with given object identity, scope, and evidence validity, the retaining model matched all expected decisions, whereas a model that resets state at scene boundaries judged 41 of 44 contradiction cases compatible with the encoded obligations. A hash-fixed, unselected enumeration of 35,042,928 event sequences found no mismatch between the model step and a separately written formula evaluator. Four reviewers of 27 natural CoC scenes agreed poorly on textual consistency (Fleiss' $\kappa$ = 0.027), so natural detection accuracy is not reported. The results show feasibility on authored symbolic inputs, not accuracy on natural annotations or vehicle safety.}
\end{kieeabstract}
\kieekeywords{\rev{Chain-of-Causation, Temporal consistency, Condition-linked obligations, Model checking, Feasibility, Evidence traceability}}
